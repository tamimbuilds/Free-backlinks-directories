# Free-backlinks-directories

Chrome Web Store (DR 100) — only relevant if your SaaS ships a browser extension. A listing here is a legitimate high-authority link, not just a directory entry.
YouTube (DR 99) — a demo or walkthrough video, linked in the description, is a real backlink most founders skip.
Pinterest (DR 97) — works best for visual or consumer products; a profile with pinned links is low effort for the DR you get.
GitHub Pages (DR 97) — host a changelog, docs site, or landing page here if any part of your product is open-source.
Vimeo (DR 96) — same logic as YouTube, smaller audience, still a real DR-96 link.
Flickr (DR 94) — mostly relevant if your product has a strong visual angle.
Medium (DR 94) — the platform you’re already publishing on; your profile and article links both count.
Substack (DR 94) — a newsletter presence here is distribution and a backlink at once.
Forbes (DR 94) — realistically requires a contributor relationship or press pickup, not a self-submission.
SourceForge (DR 93) — legacy but still authoritative; mainly relevant for open-source or downloadable software.
Quora (DR 92) — same logic as Reddit: answer real questions in your niche, link only when it fits.
Product Hunt (DR 91) — the standard startup launch platform, probably already on your radar.
Hacker News (DR 91) — earned through a genuinely interesting Show HN post or discussion, not submitted and forgotten.
Imgur (DR 91) — niche use case, mostly for visual products or launch-related memes.
Dev.to (DR 91) — a real developer blogging platform; cross-post technical content rather than just linking out.
Startup & SaaS Discovery Directories
AlternativeTo (DR 80) — list your product as an alternative to an established competitor people already search for.
SaaSHub (DR 81) — the same alternative-finder model, SaaS-focused.
AlternativeMe (DR 75) — a smaller version of the same category.
OpenAlternative (DR 60) — an alternative-finder directory specifically for open-source tools.
BetaList (DR 77) — built specifically for pre-launch and early-stage startups.
Uneed (DR 76) — general startup and tool discovery directory.
Dev Hunt (DR 64) — appears twice across your lists under slightly different capitalization; treat it as one submission, not two.
TinyLaunch (DR 73) — small-scale product launch directory.
Launch Directories (DR 45) — functions as a directory of directories; worth checking exactly what it indexes before submitting.
Startups FYI (DR 32) — niche startup listing site.
StartupBase (DR 73) — startup discovery and database site.
FoundrList (DR 72) — startup directory tied to the Foundr media brand.
TrustMRR (DR 71) — a verified-revenue directory built by indie hacker Marc Lou. Founders connect a read-only Stripe key to prove real MRR before listing — legitimate if you actually have revenue to verify, not a drive-by submission.
PeerPush (DR 75) — startup discovery and upvote platform.
There’s an AI for That (DR 77) — the well-known AI tool directory; only relevant if your product is AI-facing.
OSS Gallery (DR 33) — open-source project showcase.
No Code Founders (DR 51) — directory and community specifically for no-code builders.
Indie Hacker & Bootstrapper Communities
Indie Hackers (DR 81) — the best-known bootstrapper community; genuine participation matters more than a drive-by link.
Tiny Startups (DR 71) — community and directory for small, bootstrapped products.
SideProjectors (DR 71) — a marketplace built around buying, selling, and showcasing side projects.
SideProjects (DR 17) — a smaller, newer directory in the same space, likely a different site despite the similar name.
Bootstrappers (DR 36) — community directory for bootstrapped founders.
Indie Hacker Stacks (DR 26) — organized around what tools and stacks indie founders actually use.
Micro Founder (DR 39) — small-scale founder community and directory.
BoringCashCow (DR 28) — niche directory focused on unglamorous, profitable “boring” businesses.
Indie Page (DR 67) — another indie-product directory.
Founder Media, Interviews & Case Studies
These are harder to get than a self-submit directory, but worth more per link.

HackerNoon (DR 88) — technology blogging platform; publishing here is closer to guest-posting than directory listing.
YourStory (DR 85) — Indian startup and business media outlet.
The Hustle (DR 79) — business newsletter and media brand.
SaaStr (DR 78) — Jason Lemkin’s SaaS-focused content and community hub.
Mixergy (DR 76) — long-running business and startup interview show; getting featured takes outreach, not submission.
Foundr (DR 76) — entrepreneurship media brand.
Niche Pursuits (DR 74) — SEO and niche-site-focused blog and media outlet.
Starter Story (DR 76) — case-study interviews built around real founders and their numbers; requires being accepted, not just listed.
Failory (DR 75) — startup failure case studies; an unusual angle if you actually have a real lesson to share.
First Round (DR 82) — First Round Capital’s content arm; high bar, high authority.
Vocal Media (DR 82) — open publishing platform across many niches, not startup-specific.
Founder Reports (DR 66) — smaller founder-story publication.
SaaS Club (DR 59) — SaaS-focused podcast and community.
Developer & Software-Review Platforms
TrustRadius (DR 85) — B2B software review platform; usually needs real customers leaving reviews, not a founder self-listing.
StackShare (DR 78) — where companies publicly document their tech stack; relevant if your product is developer-facing infrastructure.
Hashnode (DR 83) — developer blogging platform, similar niche to Dev.to.
Peerlist (DR 77) — a professional network built specifically for tech and dev talent.
Software Suggest (DR 78) — software review and comparison directory.
Software World (DR 73) — similar software-directory and review model.
Startup Stash (DR 65) — curated directory of startup tools and resources.
